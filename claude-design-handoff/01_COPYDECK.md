## 4. Research direction

**Section number:** 03
**Eyebrow:** Where this is going
**H2:** The descent ends where trust becomes mathematics.

**Lead paragraph:**
> Every layer of this journey asked the same question at a lower level of abstraction — and at the bottom, trust stops being a policy, a product or a schema. It becomes a proof. My trajectory: **BEng Cybersecurity at Concordia → MMath in Computer Science (Quantum Information) at Waterloo's Institute for Quantum Computing → PhD in post-quantum cryptography.** Adversarial machine learning is the starting point — Carlini's territory, learning precisely how intelligent systems break. Waterloo IQC is the bridge — the one institution whose faculty spans every problem I intend to work on. Three thesis problems define the destination.

**The three thesis problems (numbered, presented as the Research section's core — the reveal):**

1. **Compact post-quantum authentication for internet protocols.**
Key exchange is solved — ML-KEM is standardized. Authentication is the bottleneck: at 2,400+ bytes, ML-DSA signatures strain TLS and break DNSSEC's constraints. The problem is a four-way constraint — compactness, proven security, implementation safety, protocol compatibility — and whoever solves it changes the infrastructure of the internet.

2. **Lattice diversification and code-based primitives.**
Nearly everything being deployed — post-quantum encryption, fully homomorphic encryption, most zero-knowledge proofs — rests on the same family of lattice assumptions. If it falls, there is no Plan B. Building efficient code-based alternatives is the insurance policy for digital civilization, and almost nobody is writing it.

3. **Formal security composition of hybrid PQC + QKD.**
Quantum key distribution needs a post-quantum authenticated channel; post-quantum systems increasingly sit beside QKD hardware. Composing the two securely is unresolved — and it sits on a geopolitical fault line, with the US and UK skeptical of QKD while the EU and China invest. A composable security framework for hybrid systems is where cryptography meets the real world I came from.

**Closing line:**
> I spent ten years in the industries that run on trust — healthcare, pharmacy, regulated data. Those are exactly the systems that will need post-quantum migration the hardest and the soonest. The first decade was the evidence. The next one is the proof.
